DA-0017 Film · 1961

The Innocents

Jack Clayton · United Kingdom

Dread 4/5

The finest adaptation of The Turn of the Screw, and one of the most beautiful ghost stories on film.

Miss Giddens, an inexperienced governess, takes charge of two orphaned children, Flora and Miles, at a large country house. The children are charming and polite. Then Miss Giddens begins to see a man on the tower, and a woman standing by the lake.

Jack Clayton’s film adapts Henry James’s novella (catalogued here as DA-0012), with a screenplay co-written by Truman Capote. Deborah Kerr plays the governess with growing intensity, and Freddie Francis’s black-and-white cinematography darkens the edges of the frame until the corners of every room seem to hide something.

Like the book, the film never settles whether the ghosts are real or whether the governess is the danger. Its daylight scenes are as frightening as its night ones, and a figure standing in the reeds in bright sunshine is as eerie as anything cinema has produced.
