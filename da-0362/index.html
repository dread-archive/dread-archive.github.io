DA-0362 Folklore · 1985

The Crying Boy

Traditional (modern legend) · United Kingdom

Dread 3/5

A mass-produced painting of a weeping child, said to survive house fires that destroy everything around it.

In September 1985, a British tabloid newspaper reported that firefighters kept finding the same print in burned-out houses: a painting of a young boy with tears on his cheeks, often completely undamaged while everything else was destroyed. Readers wrote in with their own stories. The newspaper organised mass bonfires of the prints.

The paintings, by an Italian artist, had been sold in huge numbers in department stores. Later investigations suggested that the prints were treated with a fire-resistant varnish and hung by string that burned through, so they fell face-down and were protected.

It is a modern curse built from a common object, which is why it spread so fast. Many people had one on the wall.
