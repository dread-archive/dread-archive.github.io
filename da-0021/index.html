DA-0021 Book · 1866

The Signal-Man

Charles Dickens · England

Dread 4/5

Dickens's best ghost story: a lonely railway signalman keeps receiving a warning he cannot understand.

A traveller walking along the top of a railway cutting calls down to the signalman working at the bottom, beside the mouth of a tunnel. The signalman is intelligent, careful and afraid. He explains that a figure has appeared by the red danger light, one arm over its face, the other waving, and that each time it appears, a disaster follows on the line. His signal bell also rings, he says, when no one has rung it. The traveller cannot hear it.

The story first appeared in a Christmas number of Dickens’s magazine in 1866, the year after he survived a serious rail crash himself.

It is a story about receiving a message with no way to reply or understand it in time. The ending is quick and exact, and the last sentence rearranges everything before it.
