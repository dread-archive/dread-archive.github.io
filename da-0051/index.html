DA-0051 Book · 1886

Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

Robert Louis Stevenson · United Kingdom

Dread 3/5

A mystery about a respectable man and his repellent friend, whose solution is now so famous it is hard to read the book fresh.

Mr. Utterson, a London lawyer, is disturbed by his friend Dr. Jekyll’s will, which leaves everything to a man named Edward Hyde. Hyde is small and violent, and people who meet him describe a deformity they cannot name. Utterson sets out to understand why a respected doctor would protect such a man.

Stevenson’s short novel was written quickly and became an immediate success. Modern readers know the answer before they start. The book was written as a mystery, told mostly through other people’s accounts, with the truth arriving only in the final chapter, a written statement in Jekyll’s own words.

The story’s lasting power is its idea of a second self: someone who uses your hands, your house and your signature, and whom you begin to let out on purpose.
