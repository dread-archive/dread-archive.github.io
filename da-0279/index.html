DA-0279 Game · 2013

Amnesia: A Machine for Pigs

The Chinese Room; directed by Dan Pinchbeck · United Kingdom

Dread 3/5

On New Year's Eve 1899, a London industrialist wakes from a fever and searches his house for his missing twin sons.

Oswald Mandus, a wealthy owner of a meat-processing plant, wakes in his London mansion with no memory of the last months. His two young sons are missing. A voice on the telephone tells him they are trapped below, in the machine, and he goes down through his house to find them.

The Chinese Room, known for the narrative game Dear Esther, made it as an indirect sequel to Frictional Games’ Amnesia: The Dark Descent, which published it. It removed some of the original’s survival systems and focused on story.

It is a slower, sadder game than its predecessor. Its writing, its organ-like score and its imagery of industrial London, at the end of one century and the beginning of another, make it a horror about progress and what it consumes.
