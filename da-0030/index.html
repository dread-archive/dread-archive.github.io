DA-0030 Film · 1960

Peeping Tom

Michael Powell · United Kingdom

Dread 5/5

A killer who films his victims' fear, in the film that nearly ended its director's career.

Mark Lewis is a shy young man who works as a focus puller at a London film studio and takes photographs on the side. He is also a murderer, and he films his victims as they die, using a device that forces them to watch their own fear. As a child, he was the subject of his father’s experiments on fear, which his father recorded on film.

Critics in 1960 were disgusted, and the reaction badly damaged Michael Powell’s career in Britain. Decades later, the film was championed by filmmakers and critics, and it is now considered one of the most important British films of its time.

It is a film about watching, and it implicates the watcher. Every scene asks why we look, and what looking takes from the person looked at.
