DA-0106 Film · 1983

Videodrome

David Cronenberg · Canada

Dread 4/5

A film about television that has become a film about everything that followed it.

Max Renn runs a small Toronto cable channel that specialises in sensational programming. His engineer picks up a pirate signal showing a program called Videodrome: grim, unscripted, and apparently real. Max wants it for his channel. The more he watches, the more his own body and perceptions begin to change.

David Cronenberg’s film is dense, strange and ahead of its time. Its media theorist, Professor Brian O’Blivion, appears only as a recorded image on a screen. Its most famous line, “Long live the new flesh,” has outlived the technology the film was worried about.

It is body horror, but its real subject is the relationship between watcher and signal. The question it asks is still open: whether you are watching the broadcast, or the broadcast is quietly rewriting you.
