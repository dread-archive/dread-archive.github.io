DA-0039 Book · 1948

The Lottery

Shirley Jackson · United States

Dread 5/5

A summer morning, a village square, and a custom nobody can remember the reason for.

On a clear June morning, the villagers gather in the square for the annual lottery. The children collect stones. The men talk about tractors and rain. Everyone is a little impatient to get it over with.

When The New Yorker published the story in 1948, it received a flood of letters, many of them angry or confused, and some asking where such lotteries were held. Jackson’s plain, friendly style is exactly what makes it land.

The story is not interested in explaining the lottery. That is the point. Everyone follows the ritual because everyone has always followed it, and nobody wants to be the one who asks why.
