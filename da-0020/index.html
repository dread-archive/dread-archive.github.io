DA-0020 Book · 1928

The Call of Cthulhu

H. P. Lovecraft · United States

Dread 3/5

The story that started a mythology, told as a man sorting through a dead relative's files.

A man inherits the papers of his great-uncle, a professor, and begins to sort them. There is a strange clay bas-relief made by a young artist after a fever dream, notes on a police raid on a cult in the Louisiana swamps, and a newspaper clipping about a sailor who survived something in the South Pacific. Separately, each item means little. Together they point to a single thing, asleep beneath the sea, and to the people who dream of it.

The story opens by saying that the greatest mercy in the world is that the human mind cannot connect everything it knows. Then the narrator connects it.

Lovecraft’s prose can be heavy going, and his wider views were ugly. But the structure of this story, dread assembled from an archive, was new, and it has been copied ever since.
