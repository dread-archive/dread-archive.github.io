DA-0023 Book · 1895

The King in Yellow

Robert W. Chambers · United States

Dread 3/5

A story collection built around a play that drives its readers mad, and one of weird fiction's great ideas.

The first four stories in Robert W. Chambers’s collection share a background object: a play called The King in Yellow. Its first act is harmless. Anyone who reads the second act is changed, and not for the better. Characters mention the Yellow Sign, the lost city of Carcosa and the Pallid Mask, and the text of the play is never given.

Chambers borrowed some of those names from Ambrose Bierce, and his stories were in turn borrowed by H. P. Lovecraft and many writers since. The collection returned to wide attention in 2014, when a popular television crime drama used its imagery.

The idea is simple and strong: a text as a contagion, dangerous not because of what it shows but because of what it does to whoever reads it. The collection’s later stories are ordinary romances. Most readers stop after the fourth.
