DA-0372 Film · 2020

The Invisible Man

Leigh Whannell · United States / Australia

Dread 4/5

A woman escapes her controlling partner. After his death, she becomes convinced he is still in the room.

Cecilia escapes in the night from the house of her abusive boyfriend, a wealthy scientist who works on optics. Soon after, she learns he has killed himself and left her money. Then small things happen: a knife is gone from the kitchen, a pan catches fire, a breath appears in the cold air. She is sure he is still there, watching. Nobody believes her.

Leigh Whannell’s reinvention of H. G. Wells’s story turns it into a film about coercive control and not being believed. Elisabeth Moss makes the audience feel her fear and isolation, while the camera lingers on empty corners of rooms.

Its best scenes are shots of nothing at all: an empty chair, an open doorway. You find yourself searching them, which is exactly the point.
