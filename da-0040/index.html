DA-0040 Film · 1960

Black Sunday

Mario Bava · Italy

Dread 4/5

A gothic vampire tale in gorgeous black and white, and the debut of Italian horror's great stylist.

In seventeenth-century Moldavia, a witch is executed by having a spiked mask hammered onto her face, and she curses her brother’s descendants. Two centuries later, two travelling doctors disturb her tomb. A drop of blood falls on her remains, and she begins to return, seeking to take the life of a young descendant who looks exactly like her.

Mario Bava was a celebrated cinematographer before he directed, and this first film is a showcase of fog, candlelight, crypts and slow camera movement. The film was loosely based on Nikolai Gogol’s story Viy. Gogol is also the author of Diary of a Madman (DA-0011).

The opening execution was cut in several countries, and the film was banned in the United Kingdom for years. Its influence on gothic horror, and on Barbara Steele’s career as its face, was immediate.
