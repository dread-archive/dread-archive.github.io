DA-0003 Book · 1818

Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus

Mary Shelley · United Kingdom

Dread 3/5

The first great horror story about a made mind, and still the saddest.

Victor Frankenstein assembles a body, gives it life, looks at it once, and runs away. The rest of the novel is the cost of that look.

Mary Shelley began the story during a ghost-story contest at the Villa Diodati in 1816, and the book was first published anonymously. Most adaptations remember the laboratory and the lightning. The novel is more interested in what happens afterwards. The creature teaches itself to read. It learns language by listening through a wall. It asks its maker for one thing, a companion, and is refused.

It is often read as a warning about science. It reads just as well as a story about responsibility. Nobody asks to be made. The creature’s real horror is not its face. It is how clearly it can explain what was done to it.
