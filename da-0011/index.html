DA-0011 Book · 1835

Diary of a Madman

Nikolai Gogol · Russia

Dread 3/5

A lowly clerk's diary that begins with office gossip and ends in a calendar that no longer exists.

Aksenty Poprishchin is a minor civil servant in St. Petersburg. His job is sharpening quills for the director of his department, with whose daughter he is hopelessly in love. He keeps a diary. In it, he notes that he has overheard two dogs talking to each other, and that he intends to read their letters.

Gogol’s story is funny for its first half and harrowing by its end. Poprishchin’s entries grow stranger. He becomes convinced that he is the rightful King of Spain. The dates at the top of his entries give way, first to impossible months, then to no date at all.

It is one of the first great portraits of a mind recording its own collapse, and it has lent its title to many works since, including a famous rock album. The horror is in the handwriting. Each entry sounds perfectly reasonable to the person writing it.
