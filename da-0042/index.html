DA-0042 Book · 1907

The Willows

Algernon Blackwood · United Kingdom

Dread 4/5

Two men camp on an island in the Danube, and slowly realise they are somewhere they were not meant to be.

Two friends are travelling down the Danube by canoe. Past Vienna, where the river spreads into a wilderness of marsh and shifting sandbanks, they stop for the night on a small island covered in willows. The wind is loud. The river is rising. The willows seem to be moving in a way the wind does not explain.

Blackwood’s novella contains almost no events. There are sounds in the night, marks in the sand, a hole in the canoe, and a growing conviction that the two men have strayed into a place where some other order of being is close to the surface, and has noticed them.

H. P. Lovecraft considered it the finest weird tale in English. Its horror is vast and indifferent rather than cruel, and its best trick is to make an ordinary landscape feel like it is paying attention.
