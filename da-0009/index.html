DA-0009 Film · 1920

The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari

Robert Wiene · Germany

Dread 3/5

A nightmare built from painted angles, and the film that taught cinema to show a mind instead of a place.

At a travelling fair in a small town, a showman called Dr. Caligari presents Cesare, a sleepwalker who, he says, has slept for twenty-three years and can answer any question. A young man asks how long he has to live. Cesare answers.

The film’s sets are painted canvas: crooked streets, slanted windows, shadows drawn straight onto the walls. Nothing is level. It is the defining work of German Expressionist cinema, and its look still shapes how films show madness.

It is also told as a story within a story, by a narrator who may not be reliable. The painted world is not decoration. It is the view from inside someone’s head.
