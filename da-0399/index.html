DA-0399 Folklore · 1977

The Enfield Poltergeist

Reported case (Enfield, London) · United Kingdom

Dread 3/5

A 1970s council house, a single mother and her children, and one of the most famous poltergeist cases ever recorded.

In 1977, a single mother living in a council house in Enfield, north London, reported that furniture was moving on its own and that there were knocks on the walls. The events centred on her children, especially her eleven-year-old daughter. Investigators from the Society for Psychical Research, Maurice Grosse and Guy Lyon Playfair, spent many months at the house.

They recorded knocking, moving objects and, most famously, a gruff voice that appeared to speak through the girl. Sceptics suspected the children of hoaxing, and the girls themselves later said some of the events had been faked, though not all.

The case was the basis of later dramas and films. It lasts because of its ordinariness: a cramped house, tired adults, tape recorders on the kitchen table, and a voice nobody could agree on.
