DA-0224 Game · 2002

Eternal Darkness: Sanity's Requiem

Silicon Knights · Canada

Dread 4/5

A horror game that attacks the player as well as the character, with tricks that still work today.

Alexandra Roivas investigates her grandfather’s gruesome death in his Rhode Island mansion and finds a book bound in skin, the Tome of Eternal Darkness. Reading it, she plays through the lives of people across two thousand years who have encountered the same ancient powers.

Released on the Nintendo GameCube, the game is best known for its sanity meter. As the character’s sanity drops, the game begins to lie to the player. The volume seems to turn itself down. The screen suggests the console has been switched off, or the controller unplugged. Most famously, it pretends to delete the saved game.

It is one of the clearest examples of horror breaking the fourth wall. The fear is not only for the character on screen. It is the moment when you doubt what your own machine is telling you.
