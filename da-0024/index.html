DA-0024 Book · 1843

The Tell-Tale Heart

Edgar Allan Poe · United States

Dread 3/5

A confession disguised as a defence, from a narrator who wants you to know how reasonable he is.

The narrator wants you to understand that he is not mad. He has murdered an old man he claims to have loved, because of the man’s pale, filmed eye, and he is telling you how carefully he did it, as proof of how sane he is.

Poe’s story is only a few pages long and is one of the most imitated in literature. Almost nothing happens except the telling. The horror is in the voice: precise, proud, and getting faster.

It is the model for every story told by someone who protests too much. The more the narrator insists that he is calm, the louder the sound under the floorboards becomes.
