DA-0018 Film · 1963

The Haunting

Robert Wise · United Kingdom / United States

Dread 4/5

The best adaptation of Hill House: a haunted-house film that frightens without ever showing you anything.

Robert Wise’s adaptation of Shirley Jackson’s novel (DA-0007) follows the same small party: an anthropologist studying the paranormal, a lonely woman named Eleanor who has spent her life caring for her mother, the sharp and playful Theodora, and the house’s future heir. They come to Hill House to see whether it is haunted. It is, and it wants Eleanor.

Wise shot in black and white with distorting wide-angle lenses, so the rooms seem to lean in. The famous scenes are almost entirely sound: a pounding that moves down the corridor, a door that bulges as if breathing, a voice behind the wall. Nothing is ever shown.

It remains a lesson most haunted-house films ignore. The audience’s imagination will always build a worse monster than the effects department can.
