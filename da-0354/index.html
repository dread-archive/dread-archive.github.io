DA-0354 Folklore · 1518

The Dancing Plague of 1518

Historical event · Strasbourg (then Holy Roman Empire)

Dread 3/5

In a summer in Strasbourg, people began to dance in the streets and could not stop.

In July 1518, a woman in Strasbourg began to dance in the street and continued for days. Others joined her. Within weeks, many people were dancing, some of them until they collapsed. The city authorities, believing the dancers needed to dance it out, reportedly provided space and even musicians, which seems to have made things worse.

The event is recorded in documents of the time, including physicians’ notes and city records. Modern historians have suggested stress-induced mass hysteria, in a population suffering famine and disease, and shaped by belief in a curse linked to a saint.

It is one of the strangest documented events in European history. The horror is not in anything supernatural, but in a body that has stopped listening.
