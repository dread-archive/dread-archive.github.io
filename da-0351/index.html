DA-0351 Folklore · 1767

The Beast of Gévaudan

Historical accounts · France

Dread 3/5

A creature that killed people across a region of southern France in the 1760s. Its nature is still argued about.

Between 1764 and 1767, a series of attacks in the Gévaudan region of southern France killed many people, mostly women and children tending animals in the fields. Witnesses described a large wolf-like animal with a reddish coat and a long tail. The king sent hunters and soldiers. Several large wolves were killed, and each time the attacks continued.

The killings ended after a local hunter shot a large animal in 1767. What it was remains debated: wolves, a hybrid, an escaped exotic animal, or several animals blamed as one.

The case is well documented, and it became a legend while it was still happening. It has inspired novels, films and endless theories, because the one thing everyone agrees on is that the people were real.
