DA-0027 Book · 1839

The Fall of the House of Usher

Edgar Allan Poe · United States

Dread 4/5

A gloomy house, a dying family, and a crack in the wall that runs all the way through the story.

The narrator is summoned by his childhood friend Roderick Usher to the Usher family house, a decaying building beside a dark lake. Roderick is sick with nerves and dread. His twin sister Madeline is wasting away from a mysterious illness.

Poe builds the story from a single mood. Roderick paints abstract pictures, plays strange music and believes the house itself is alive. When Madeline appears to die, the two men place her body in a vault beneath the building. Then the sounds begin.

The house and the family are the same thing in the story, and when one fails, so does the other. It is short, dense and theatrical, and its final image, a crack in the wall widening under a blood-red moon, is one of the most famous in American fiction.
