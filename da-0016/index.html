DA-0016 Book · 1918

A Madman's Diary

Lu Xun · China

Dread 4/5

A diary of paranoia that turns out to be an accusation, and a landmark of modern Chinese literature.

The story opens with a short preface. The narrator visits an old school friend and learns that the friend’s younger brother has been ill but has recovered and gone away to take an official post. He is shown the diary the brother kept while he was ill.

The diary that follows is written by a man convinced that everyone around him, his neighbours, the doctor, even his own brother, is planning to eat him. He reads the old books of his village and finds, between the lines of every page, the same words.

Published in 1918 in the magazine New Youth, it is widely considered the first major modern story written in vernacular Chinese. It is a horror story and a political allegory at once. Its madman may be the only person who sees the society around him clearly. Its last line is a plea: save the children.
