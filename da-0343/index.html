DA-0343 Folklore · 1577

Black Shuck

Traditional (East Anglia) · England

Dread 3/5

A huge black dog with burning eyes, said to haunt the coast and lanes of East Anglia.

In the folklore of Norfolk and Suffolk, Black Shuck is an enormous black dog, sometimes with a single eye, sometimes with eyes like burning coals, that walks the coast paths and country lanes at night. To meet it is, in many versions, a sign of death within the year.

The most famous story dates from a violent thunderstorm in August 1577, when a black dog was said to have burst into churches at Bungay and Blythburgh during services, killing worshippers. Scorch marks on a church door at Blythburgh are still pointed out as the marks of its claws.

Black dog legends are found all over Britain and are often said to have inspired Arthur Conan Doyle’s The Hound of the Baskervilles. Shuck is the most famous of them: a shape in the dark that keeps pace with you, and never comes closer.
