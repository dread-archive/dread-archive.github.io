DA-0010 Book · 1887

Le Horla

Guy de Maupassant · France

Dread 4/5

A diary kept by a man who slowly realises he is not alone in his own house, and cannot prove it.

A man living comfortably beside the Seine in Normandy salutes a Brazilian ship as it passes his house. Soon afterwards, he begins to sleep badly. He wakes exhausted, as if something had been pressing on him. Then the water in the carafe by his bed starts to disappear during the night.

Maupassant tells the story as the man’s diary. He runs experiments. He leaves out water, milk, wine and bread, and only the water and milk are gone in the morning. He becomes certain that an invisible being has arrived, one that feeds on him and can control him. He names it the Horla.

It is one of the great stories of a mind watching itself come apart, and it is told with terrible calm. The reader never gets to know what is real. There is only a diary, and a narrator who is sure of everything except himself.
