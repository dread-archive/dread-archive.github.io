DA-0353 Folklore · 1872

The Mary Celeste

Historical event · International (Atlantic)

Dread 3/5

A merchant ship found sailing on its own in the Atlantic, in good condition, with no one aboard.

In December 1872, the crew of another ship found the Mary Celeste drifting in the Atlantic between the Azores and Portugal. It was seaworthy. Its cargo was nearly all intact. There were supplies of food and water. The lifeboat was missing, and so were the captain, his wife, their small daughter and the entire crew. They were never found.

An inquiry could not explain what had happened. A young Arthur Conan Doyle later wrote a fictional account that added invented details, such as half-eaten meals on the table, and these became part of the legend.

The real facts are strange enough: a ship abandoned in a hurry, for a reason that seemed urgent at the time, and a silence that has lasted over a century.
