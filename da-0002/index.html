DA-0002 Film · 1932

Vampyr

Carl Theodor Dreyer · Germany / France

Dread 4/5

A vampire film that plays like a half-remembered dream, and the strangest of the early horror classics.

A young traveller, Allan Gray, arrives at a village inn and is drawn into the affairs of a nearby manor where a woman is wasting away. The explanation, when it comes, is a vampire. But the film is barely interested in explanations. Shadows move without the people who cast them. A doctor’s poison bottle becomes a clue. In the most famous sequence, Gray sees himself lying in a coffin and the camera takes his place, looking up through a small window as the lid is closed and he is carried out.

Carl Theodor Dreyer loosely based the story on Sheridan Le Fanu’s collection In a Glass Darkly, the book that also contains Carmilla (DA-0036). He cast mostly non-professionals, including the film’s financier in the lead, and shot through a soft, washed-out haze that makes every scene look like early morning.

It was a commercial failure on release. It has been admired ever since for the thing that failed it: it refuses to wake up.
