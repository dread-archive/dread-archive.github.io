DA-0004 Book · 1842

The Masque of the Red Death

Edgar Allan Poe · United States

Dread 3/5

A short, perfectly built story about the wealthy trying to lock death out, and death arriving at the party anyway.

A plague called the Red Death is killing the country. Prince Prospero gathers a thousand friends inside a fortified abbey, welds the gates shut, and holds a masked ball while the world outside dies. The abbey’s suite of seven rooms is decorated in seven colours. The last is black, lit through blood-red glass, and holds a great ebony clock whose chimes make the dancers stop.

At midnight a new guest appears, masked as a corpse of the Red Death. Prospero pursues him through all seven rooms.

Poe wrote it as a tableau more than a tale: every room, every colour, every chime has a meaning the reader can decode. Its lesson needs no decoding. You cannot wall out what is coming. You can only choose which room you are in when it arrives.
