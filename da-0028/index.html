DA-0028 Film · 1964

Onibaba

Kaneto Shindo · Japan

Dread 4/5

Two women survive a civil war by killing stray soldiers in a sea of tall grass. Then a mask arrives.

In fourteenth-century Japan, with war raging, a woman and her daughter-in-law live in a hut surrounded by tall, swaying grass. They survive by ambushing wounded or lost samurai, killing them, selling their armour, and dropping the bodies into a deep pit. When a neighbour returns from the war, desire and jealousy split the two women apart.

Then a masked samurai comes through the grass.

Kaneto Shindo shot almost the entire film among the reeds, which whisper and bend constantly around the characters. It is harsh, sensual and blunt about hunger in every sense. The demon mask at its centre became one of the defining images of Japanese horror, and its final moments are as frightening as anything the genre has produced.
